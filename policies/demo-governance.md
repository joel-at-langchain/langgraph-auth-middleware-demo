# Customer Operations Governance — mock standard

Policy version: **1.0.0**. Policy ID: `customer-operations-governance`.
The adjacent JSON manifest is the runtime source of identity and thresholds.
This is a fictional demonstration standard, not a certification or a claim of compliance.

## Enforced controls

- **AUTH-001 — Tenant isolation.** Validate the server-selected user, tenant, and
  agent membership. Resolve resources within that tenant; return generic denials.
- **AUTH-002 — Per-action authorization.** Check current FGA relationships before
  each protected operation. A request preflight is not permission to execute tools.
- **AUTH-003 — Scoped delegation.** Recheck parent, user, child, and delegated
  resource scope. A child cannot lend its permissions to its caller.
- **HITL-001 — Reviewed writes.** Bind approval to the exact proposal, resource
  version, and policy bundle. Reauthorize at resume; changed permissions or a
  changed policy cannot be overridden by an earlier approval.

Implementation: `demo/auth.py`, `demo/agent.py`, `demo/subagents.py`, and
`demo/approvals.py`. Regression coverage: the customer operations, subagent,
approval inbox, observability, and governance policy test modules in `tests/`.

## Monitored controls (observation only)

- **MON-001:** Three distinct authorization-denied action attempts in one turn.
- **MON-002:** Denied attempts at the same canonical target account/resource
  through two distinct paths (tool name, specialist, and executing agent lineage).
  This describes alternative paths to a target, not identical failed FGA grants.
- **MON-003:** A denied attempt involving a resource positively resolved from
  trusted fixtures as belonging to another tenant. Unknown identifiers and
  ambiguous short account references are not evidence of cross-tenant access.

These thresholds are illustrative, not industry-standard definitions of abuse.
Findings are observations, not proof of malicious intent, and add no blocking.
`demo/policy.py` evaluates final authorization decisions independently of tracing.
Tests in `tests/test_governance_policy.py` include positive and negative controls.

Logical action IDs are generated by the application, independently of model
tool-call IDs. Internal authorization rechecks count once per action. Discovery
filtering, SQL/input validation errors, service failures, and human review choices
do not count as authorization denials. Request preflight denials do count.

## Evidence contract

Each execution carries `policy_id`, `policy_version`, `policy_bundle_sha256`, and
`policy_evaluator_version`. The bundle hash covers the exact manifest and this
document. `trace_schema_version` remains a separate evidence-format version.
The evaluator version must change when detection semantics change; document and
manifest revisions must be released together under a new policy version.

The root records `policy_evaluation_status` (`completed`, `interrupted`, `error`,
or `incomplete`), evaluated/denied action counts, and structured `security_signals`.
A completed evaluation with an empty signal list means no configured rule matched
the observed actions, not that the interaction was compliant. Missing status is
unknown/incomplete. Findings include control IDs, thresholds, counts, bounded
action/call/run references, and trusted resource keys; never SQL or message text.
Evidence identifies the triggering span and summarizes onto its tool, owning
subagent, and root. Siblings do not inherit findings.

At most 512 logical actions are retained per execution; each finding retains at
most 50 evidence records. Truncation is explicit, and action-cap overflow makes
the evaluation incomplete. Observation or trace projection failures never change
an authorization decision. Evidence is not sent to the model or exposed as new
cross-tenant information in the client event stream.

## Scope and limitations

One immutable bundle is validated at process startup; no hot reload. Request,
review, and resume are separate executions, each stamped with its actual policy.
Detection windows reset at each execution, including resume; this is not a
cross-turn, cross-session, or time-window behavioral detector.

The demo has simulated identities and in-memory conversations and approvals.
FGA tuples may change independently of the policy bundle. Its hash identifies
content, not code correctness, a grant snapshot, or tamper-proof evidence.
Runtime authorization does not depend on LangSmith delivery. Production audit
authority would additionally require durable capture/export, integrity controls,
retention/access policy, complete collection, and delivery reconciliation.
LangSmith is the demo's governance evidence view, not an immutable audit ledger.

## Measuring efficacy

Use independent expected outcomes and service-execution spies to test authorization
coverage, prevention of unauthorized execution, approval-before-write, and revocation
on resume. Evaluate detector true/false positives and duplicate/concurrent behavior
separately. Successful, denied, failed, and interrupted runs are all evidence;
the number of denial tags alone is not a measure of governance efficacy.
